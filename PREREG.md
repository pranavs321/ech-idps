# Study Preregistration: Encrypted Traffic Malware Detection & Shortcut Learning Evaluation

## Research Question
Can JA4/TLS + flow features detect malware in encrypted traffic, and does near-perfect performance survive when shortcut learning and environmental artifacts are removed?

---

## Evaluation Protocols (P1–P8)
- **P1 (random-flow):** Random flow-level split across the dataset (standard published benchmark).
- **P2 (capture-disjoint):** Disjoint split by capture file (PCAP), evaluating generalisation to unseen capture files.
- **P3 (JA4-disjoint):** Disjoint split by JA4 fingerprint, evaluating generalisation to unseen client TLS signatures.
- **P4 (family-disjoint):** Disjoint split by malware family or application name, evaluating generalisation to unseen malware.
- **P5 (temporal):** Temporal split (training on data $\le 2023$, testing on $2024 \to 2025\text{--}2026$), evaluating resistance to concept drift over time.
- **P6 (environment-disjoint):** Disjoint split by environment/platform (desktop $\leftrightarrow$ mobile, corpus $\leftrightarrow$ corpus), testing for lab/network shortcut learning.
- **P7 (matched-environment):** Evaluation on matched-environment captures where both malicious and benign flows were recorded on the same host/network.
- **P8 (counterfactual grid):** Factorial interventional grid (OS $\times$ Client $\times$ Behaviour $\times$ TLS Version) testing frozen models.

---

## Models and Baselines

### Baselines
- **B0 (Always-Malware):** Trivial floor baseline predicting the majority class.
- **B1 (JA4 Lookup Table):** Majority label per JA4 fingerprint on training set, reporting test coverage.
- **B2 (Environment-Only):** Model trained only on physical packet/TCP features (TTL, window scale, SYN size, largest segment).

### Models
- **M1 (Behaviour XGBoost):** XGBoost trained strictly on flow statistical features.
- **M2 (JA4 XGBoost):** XGBoost trained strictly on JA4 component features.
- **M3 (Combined XGBoost):** XGBoost trained on combined flow statistics and JA4 component features.
- **M4 (Proposed Detector):** Environment-relative grouping detector operating without un-normalized hardware/environment shortcuts.

---

## Lead Metrics & Statistical Principles
- **Primary Metrics:** Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), Precision-Recall Area Under Curve (PR-AUC), and Recall at False Positive Rate $\text{FPR} = 10^{-3}$ ($0.001$).
- **F1 Score:** F1 score is reported strictly alongside the trivial baseline F1 score ($B0$).
- **Capture-Level Evaluation:** Metrics computed at the capture level.
- **Bootstrapping:** Confidence intervals generated by bootstrapping *captures*, never individual flows.

---

## Exclusion Rules
1. **ClientHello Requirement:** JA4 metrics are evaluated exclusively on flows that contain a valid TLS ClientHello.
2. **SYN Requirement:** Flows lacking a SYN packet are excluded from environment-feature evaluations.
3. **Matched-Environment Labels:** Ambiguous or non-IOC / non-allowlisted flows are designated as gray flows and excluded from matched-environment labels.

---

## Pre-Registered Hypotheses (H1–H5)
- **H1:** Protocol P1 (random flow split) beats P3 (JA4-disjoint), P4 (family-disjoint), and P6 (environment-disjoint) by more than the bootstrap interval.
- **H2:** The environment-only baseline (B2) reaches $\text{MCC} \ge 0.5$ on $\ge 1$ public corpus under P1.
- **H3:** Under P6 (environment-disjoint), every model falls to $\text{MCC} \le 0.2$.
- **H4:** In the counterfactual grid (P8), the JA4 model's score varies more across clients than across network behaviours.
- **H5:** The proposed detector (M4) loses less MCC from P1 $\to$ P6/P7 than the combined XGBoost (M3).

---

## Deliberate Leakage Control Test
Preprocessing pipelines (e.g. scalers, encoders, feature selection) must be fit on training splits only. A deliberate leakage check—fitting preprocessing on the full dataset—is executed to confirm that the protocol runner flags and reports the resulting score inflation.
